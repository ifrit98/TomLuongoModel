Subject: A practical way to test the yen-carry / collateral model

Tom —

I listened closely to the Larry Lepard conversation and the August 9 market report and tried to translate the model into something that can be attacked with actual balance sheets, market data, event studies, and explicit failure conditions.

I am not trying to sand off the interesting part of the thesis or replace it with a generic “Japan matters” story. The purpose is the opposite: preserve the causal structure you are describing, specify exactly what would have to be true for it to work, and make each link testable enough that the data can either strengthen it or kill it.

The package has two parts:

1. A short working paper that maps the model, derives the JPY-to-EUR carry properly, separates the funding / FX-hedging / collateral / loss-absorption chains, and lays out a staged empirical program.
2. A small Python research scaffold that downloads the public baseline data, builds the panels, runs initial event studies and HAC regressions, produces charts, and writes a falsification scorecard. I included public-data snapshots so the parsers can be tested offline.

The main technical point is that the 30–35 bp German-Japanese ten-year spread is a useful diagnostic, but it is not yet the executable trade. The tradable object has to include forward points, cross-currency basis, hedge ratio and tenor, repo, duration, margin, capital, and transaction costs. Under a fully hedged covered-interest-parity trade, the apparent advantage of cheap yen funding is mostly offset in the forward market. So the thesis becomes especially strong if the actual book is partially hedged, repeatedly rolling short hedges against long assets, dependent on an adverse basis, or constrained by collateral and dealer balance sheets. Those are all measurable.

The sequence I propose testing is:

BOJ surprise / yen appreciation
→ effective JPY-funded European carry compresses
→ short-yen and European-risk positions are reduced
→ European banks / sovereigns / dealers take more stress than U.S. or Japanese equivalents
→ capital migrates toward the U.S. and/or Japan
→ policy support eases dollar-funding stress
→ gold rises while the dollar remains firm and credit conditions stabilize.

That last joint sign pattern is what would distinguish your “gold as collateral / system repair” model from a conventional dollar-collapse or indiscriminate risk-off model.

Before treating the code as a serious test, I would especially want your check on seven points:

1. What is the actual representative trade: direct JPY→EUR, JPY→USD→EUR, long Bund/short JGB, BTP carry, European bank credit, or a broader portfolio?
2. What hedge ratio and hedge tenor do you think best describe the vulnerable book?
3. Which institutions or venues are the expected loss center: UK dealers, European banks, pensions, hedge funds, sovereign books, or some combination?
4. What exact operation are you calling the recent U.S.–Japan intervention: spot FX, Fed dollar swap, ESF operation, repo/credit facility, jawboning, or multiple legs?
5. What observable would make you say “London/ECB is taking the loss” rather than merely acting as the booking or custody center?
6. What legal/accounting route do you envision for a gold revaluation to enter the monetary system, given that Treasury owns the gold and the Fed holds statutory-value gold certificates?
7. Which event dates and calls should be frozen now, before looking at the results, so we do not accidentally fit the narrative after the fact?

The note is written as a working instrument, not a takedown. Some tests are immediately runnable with free data; the decisive covered-carry and institutional-risk tests will require Bloomberg/Refinitiv or dealer-quality series. The code makes those missing inputs explicit instead of quietly substituting weaker proxies.

I would be interested in whether this captures the model you actually mean. If it does, the next step is to replace the generic European-asset leg with the trade you think is truly load-bearing, freeze the relevant events, and run the full sequence.
